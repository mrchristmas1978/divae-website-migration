How to Find the Right PIM System for Your Business

The 8 Most Important Questions to Ask When Selecting a PIM System – Including a Practical Checklist

Dorothee Haensch
Content:
  • What Is a PIM System and What Role Does It Play in E-Commerce?
  • The Most Important Criteria for Choosing a PIM System
  • Step by Step: Selecting the Right PIM System
  • Checklist: Finding the Right PIM System
  • Conclusion

Whether you're selling through an online shop, marketplace, print catalog, or sales app, product information must be accurate, consistent, and available across all channels. For many businesses, this has become a major challenge: product data is spread across multiple systems, processes have evolved over time, and every update requires manual effort. As businesses expand into new sales channels, add more languages, or grow their product portfolios, complexity increases significantly.


In addition, today's customers expect complete and reliable product information, regardless of where they interact with your brand. Inaccurate data, inconsistent descriptions, or missing information directly impact the customer experience, conversion rates, and ultimately business success.


A Product Information Management (PIM) system provides the foundation to address these challenges. It centralizes product information, standardizes processes, and ensures that every channel accesses the same reliable data source.


However, this is where the real challenge begins for many organizations: Which PIM system best fits your business requirements?


Choosing a PIM system should not be based solely on features or vendor rankings. The deciding factor is how well the solution aligns with your business processes, existing technology landscape, and long-term strategic goals. Implementing a PIM is not simply a software decision—it is a strategic investment in future-proof commerce and product experience management.

What Is a PIM System and What Role Does It Play in E-Commerce?

A Product Information Management (PIM) system is a centralized platform for managing, enriching, and distributing product information. Businesses use a PIM to consolidate all relevant product data in one place, structure it consistently, and make it available across multiple sales and marketing channels.


This includes, for example:

  • Product descriptions

  • Technical specifications

  • Pricing and variant information

  • Translations

  • Categories and classifications

  • Marketing content

  • Certificates and documentation


The goal is to create a single source of truth for all product information. Instead of maintaining data separately in Excel spreadsheets, ERP systems, or online shops, all stakeholders work from a shared, centralized database. This reduces errors, streamlines processes, and accelerates the launch of new products.

The Most Important Criteria for Choosing a PIM System

When selecting a PIM system, businesses should look beyond feature comparisons alone. A strategic evaluation based on the following eight questions leads to a far better decision.

1. What Are Your Goals for Implementing a PIM?

Many organizations start by comparing software features. A more effective approach is to define your business objectives first and evaluate potential solutions afterward.


Ask yourself questions such as:

  • Which business challenges do we want to solve?

  • Which processes currently consume the most time?

  • Where do errors or process inefficiencies occur?

  • Which departments work with product information?


Typical objectives of a PIM implementation include:

  • Faster time-to-market for new products

  • Higher product data quality

  • Improved collaboration between marketing, product management, and e-commerce teams

  • Reduced manual maintenance effort

  • Consistent product information across all sales channels


It is equally important to consider future requirements. If international expansion, new product ranges, or additional sales channels are already part of your strategy, these should be taken into account from the very beginning.

2. Will the PIM System Still Meet Your Needs in Five Years?

A PIM system should not only satisfy today's requirements but also scale with your business. Organizations with large product catalogs or international operations require a solution that can handle growing data volumes and increasing complexity.


Key considerations include:

  • Growing product portfolio: The PIM solution must remain performant even as the number of products increases. Flexible data models allow new product groups, attributes, and variants to be added without extensive customization.

  • International markets and multilingual support: Companies operating globally need capabilities for translations, regional variations, and market-specific product information.

  • New sales channels: Digital commerce continues to evolve. Beyond traditional online stores, marketplaces, mobile apps, social commerce, and digital services are becoming increasingly important.

3. Does the PIM Fit Your Existing Technology Landscape?

A PIM delivers the greatest value when it becomes part of an integrated digital ecosystem. Therefore, businesses should carefully evaluate how well the solution integrates with existing systems.


Important integrations include:

  • ERP systems: Enable automated synchronization of product and master data, reducing manual work and minimizing errors.

  • E-commerce platforms: Ensure that accurate and enriched product information is always available in online stores.

  • Content Management Systems (CMS): Support consistent presentation of product information alongside editorial content.

  • Digital Asset Management (DAM): Connect images, videos, and other digital assets directly to products.

  • Marketplaces: Allow product information to be adapted and distributed efficiently to external sales platforms.

  • CRM systems: Link product information with customer data to support personalized experiences and sales processes.

4. Can Business Users Work Independently with the PIM?

A PIM system is used daily by multiple departments. Marketing creates product content, product management maintains attributes, e-commerce teams optimize content for sales channels, and IT manages operations and integrations.


The more intuitive the system is, the faster business users can become productive and the less dependent they are on IT.


A modern PIM should provide:

  • An intuitive user interface

  • Role-based permissions

  • Flexible workflows

  • Transparent approval processes

  • Easy bulk editing

  • Customized dashboards for different user groups


Especially in international organizations or companies with extensive product catalogs, usability often determines whether a PIM is widely adopted and consistently used.

5. How Strong Are Your Data Quality and Product Data Governance Processes?

A PIM system alone does not automatically improve product data quality. Success depends on the processes surrounding the system. Before selecting a solution, businesses should define how Product Data Governance will be implemented. Strong governance ensures that product information remains accurate, complete, and consistent.


Key elements include:

  • Standardized data structures

  • Defined mandatory fields

  • Data quality validation

  • Approval workflows

  • Clearly defined responsibilities and governance processes

6. Can AI Features Help Automate Your Processes?

Modern PIM systems go far beyond simply storing product data. They automate repetitive tasks while helping businesses maintain high data quality.


Especially for organizations managing large product portfolios, automation and AI capabilities can significantly increase productivity. They reduce manual effort, minimize errors, and accelerate processes—from data enrichment to publication.

AI in Product Information Management


Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly valuable component of modern PIM software. Rather than replacing human expertise, AI supports teams by taking over repetitive and time-consuming tasks.


Typical use cases include:

  • Rule-based data validation

  • Bulk editing of product information

  • Automatic channel assignment

  • Workflow automation

  • Generating initial product descriptions from structured data

  • Translation and localization support

  • Automatic product classification

  • Identifying missing or inconsistent information

  • Suggestions for optimizing product titles and attributes

  • Automatic completion of missing product attributes

7. How Cost-Effective Is the PIM Solution Really?

When evaluating PIM software, many businesses focus primarily on licensing costs. However, licensing alone does not provide a complete picture. A more meaningful evaluation considers the Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)—the overall costs incurred throughout the system's lifecycle.


These costs include:

  • Licensing model: Cloud and on-premises solutions differ not only in deployment but also in cost structure. Businesses should evaluate which features are included in the license and where additional costs may arise.

  • Implementation: The implementation itself is often one of the largest investments, including consulting, solution design, system configuration, integration development, data migration, testing, quality assurance, and user training.

  • Operating costs: After go-live, ongoing expenses may include hosting, monitoring, support, and system administration.

  • Maintenance and continuous improvement: A PIM system is never truly finished. New business requirements, additional sales channels, and organizational changes require ongoing optimization and development.


The most important question is therefore not: "Which solution is the cheapest today?" Instead, ask: "Which solution will deliver the greatest long-term value for our business?"

8. Who Is the Right Implementation Partner?

Implementing a PIM system is much more than deploying software—it is a business transformation project. Alongside the technology itself, the implementation partner plays a crucial role in long-term project success.


Key selection criteria include:

  • Proven experience with PIM implementation projects

  • Industry expertise and understanding of business processes

  • Strong methodology for solution selection and implementation

  • Integration expertise for complex technology landscapes

  • Support for change management and user adoption

  • Long-term assistance with optimization and continuous development


As an experienced digital transformation partner, we support organizations not only with the technical implementation but throughout the entire journey—from strategy and solution selection to successful operation and ongoing optimization.

Step by Step: Selecting the Right PIM System

A structured selection process minimizes project risks and enables well-informed decisions.

  1. Define Business Goals and Requirements

    Start by establishing clear objectives. Which business challenges should the PIM solve? Which improvements do you expect? The answers form the basis of a prioritized requirements catalog.

  2. Involve Stakeholders Early

    Include not only IT but also all relevant business departments from the beginning. Early collaboration creates a shared understanding of requirements and increases user acceptance later in the project.

  3. Analyze Existing Processes and Data

    Before evaluating software, take a close look at your current processes and data structures. This helps identify bottlenecks, inefficient workflows, and opportunities for improvement.

  4. Evaluate Vendors Systematically

    Based on your requirements, identify suitable vendors and compare them objectively. Besides functionality, pay particular attention to integration capabilities, scalability, and deployment options.

  5. Validate Requirements Using Real Use Cases

    Instead of relying solely on product demonstrations, ask vendors to replicate real business scenarios. This provides a much clearer understanding of how well the solution supports your day-to-day processes.

  6. Conduct a Proof of Concept (PoC)

    A Proof of Concept provides additional confidence before making a final decision. It allows you to validate critical business processes under realistic conditions while identifying technical and operational risks early.

  7. Prepare the Implementation Strategically

    Even before signing the contract, planning should begin for data migration, governance, user training, and change management. Thorough preparation significantly increases the likelihood of a smooth implementation.

Checklist: Finding the Right PIM System

Before making your final decision, review the following questions:

  • Have we clearly defined our strategic objectives for implementing a PIM?

  • Which business processes should the PIM improve?

  • Which systems need to be integrated (ERP, CMS, DAM, e-commerce platform, CRM, or marketplaces)?

  • Does the solution support our future growth plans?

  • Does it offer robust multilingual and international capabilities?

  • Can business users manage product information independently?

  • Does the system support governance, workflows, and data quality management?

  • Which automation and AI capabilities are included?

  • Are the total operating costs transparent and predictable over the long term?

Conclusion

Choosing a PIM system means much more than purchasing new software. It lays the foundation for professional product information management, consistent customer experiences, and scalable commerce operations.


The best PIM solution is not necessarily the one with the longest feature list. Instead, it is the solution that best aligns with your business processes, strategic objectives, and future growth plans. Organizations that carefully evaluate data quality, governance, integration capabilities, scalability, and usability create the foundation for long-term success.


An equally important factor is following a structured selection process—from requirements analysis and real-world use case validation to a Proof of Concept. This approach minimizes project risks and protects your investment over the long term.
